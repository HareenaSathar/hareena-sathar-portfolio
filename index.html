Mapping the 2018 Kerala flood from space

After the 2018 Kerala floods I volunteered on a rehabilitation survey. Eight years later I mapped the same flood from satellite data. I compared radar change detection from Sentinel-1 with the optical MNDWI water index from Sentinel-2 over Kuttanad, the below-sea-level rice-growing wetland that was among the worst-hit areas.

  • Built the UN-SPIDER recommended-practice workflow: pre-flood and flood mosaics, speckle filtering, a backscatter-change threshold, then removal of permanent water (JRC), slopes above 5° and isolated pixels.
  • Cross-checked radar against optical with a confusion matrix on 1,000 sample points: 82% agreement, kappa 0.64.
  • Estimated impact by overlaying the flood extent on land-cover and population layers.

Thermal conductivity of geothermal aquifers: physics vs. machine learning

I reproduced a published geothermal study (Fuchs & Förster, 2010) and then tested what standard ML models could learn from the same core data. The physics reproduced almost exactly. The ML part showed something more useful: why a model that looks accurate can fail as soon as it sees a new borehole.

MAE of matrix conductivity, W m⁻¹ K⁻¹
  • Reproduced Fourier's-law heat flow (74.3 vs. published 74.2 mW m⁻²) and inverted the geometric-mean mixing law for matrix conductivity, using a Bland–Altman analysis to compare it with the published values.
  • Compared linear regression, a decision tree, SVMs, k-means and a neural network under two validation designs: random split and grouped hold-out by borehole.
  • Concluded that the missing ingredient is mineralogy (quartz and clay content) rather than model capacity, which points to wireline logs as the right predictors.
